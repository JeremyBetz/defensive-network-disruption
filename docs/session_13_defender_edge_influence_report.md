## Multi-edge findings

Segment-nearest multi-edge structure occurred in **every one of 7,227 states**
and every match. Receiver-nearest multi-edge structure occurred in equal-match
fraction **0.998833218**, with match values 0.995856354–1. “Multi-edge” means an
anonymous defender belongs to nearest blocks for at least two edges; it does not
mean they controlled or suppressed those options.

The most involved segment-nearest defender covered a mean **5.827204652 edges**
per state, with weighted 5/25/50/75/95 percentiles **3/4/6/7/9** and range 2–10.
The receiver-nearest mean was **2.826881716**, percentiles **2/2/3/3/4**, range
1–7. All nine match means for segment involvement were 5.571265678–6.141073658.

A mean **3.920664328 distinct defenders** appeared in segment-nearest blocks per
star, with percentiles 2/3/4/5/6 and range 1–9. Receiver-nearest blocks involved
mean **6.881832608**, percentiles 5/6/7/8/8 and range 2–10. Corridor relationships
were therefore repeatedly concentrated on fewer defenders than endpoint pressure.

The most involved defender's unweighted shares of segment top-1/2/3 edge
membership averaged **0.582720465**, **0.861669124** and **0.964369033**. The
secondary M1-share-weighted masses averaged **0.737451220**, **0.942971174** and
**0.990378176**. These overlays describe involvement around higher-share modeled
receiver options. They are circular rather than independent confirmation because
M1 shares already depend on defensive geometry.

## Role overlap and redundancy

Receiver-nearest and segment-nearest sets intersected on equal-match mean
**0.292810705** of edges. They were exactly equal on **0.292798036**, and the same
unique defender filled both roles at that same rate. Match-level intersection
means ranged 0.275204360–0.305245154. Numerical uniqueness under the frozen tie
rule is not a football-clear distance distinction.

The first-versus-second segment-distance gap averaged **2.298760805 m**. Its
weighted 5/25/50/75/95 percentiles were **0.840142/1.486322/2.151323/2.945999/
4.221131 m**, range 0.168457–10.074876 m. The third-versus-first gap averaged
**4.895668466 m**, with percentiles **2.164706/3.485772/4.811078/6.096944/
8.024879 m**. Every match had non-degenerate continuous variation. No football
distance threshold was introduced.

Within-star segment support-set Jaccard overlap averaged **0.388993763** for
top-1, **0.422443047** for top-2 and **0.479997747** for top-3. These patterns
appeared in all nine matches. They establish recurring shared correspondence
and graded redundancy beyond separate scalar minima. Identity-retaining nearest
records already recover top-1 sharing; the full map adds non-nearest ranks,
distances, gaps and higher-k overlap.

## Interpretation and limitations

In spent development geometry, defenders repeatedly occupy positions related to
several carrier→receiver edges, and endpoint and corridor relationships differ.
This does not show that geometry caused a receiver choice or represents perceptual
occlusion. Attacking structure and defensive placement are jointly observed.
Anonymous state-local indices prevent player attribution.

The relation matrix adds no information beyond complete state geometry. The A
classification follows from prevalence, cross-match consistency, role difference,
gap variation and top-k overlap—not formal information loss or M1 weighting.
No functional substitutability, player quality, defender value, tactical intent,
pressing quality or ownership is inferred.

Future architecture remains conceptual:
`defender-edge geometry → bounded occlusion hypothesis → option-network reshaping
→ optional threat/value weighting → defensive-configuration evaluation`.
A possible later sister-project link is pre-pass option funneling followed by a
pass and localized defensive reallocation. No sister-project data, code, result
or parameter entered this session; no pressing-trap claim follows.
